## Why no files in this post

The structure comes from the **Vr-pits "A10C Full" plan set by FLIM**: DXFs plus a FreeCAD model of the seat, tub, consoles and glare shield. It's a paid product. I won't post his files, or anything I made from them: my assembly guides, part lists, printed replacement parts keyed to his geometry, or dimensions. The Warthog Project bought the same plans and takes the same line. You can reach FLIM through the ED forums thread linked on the [about page](/about/#credits).

What I *can* share is what the build taught me.

## How it went

- **Cutting.** Everything was cut on the router in the millz shop: birch ply for the structural parts and 1/4 in A/C plywood for the thin ones. The plans call for 1/4 in MDF there, but my local supplier didn't think quarter-inch MDF was even a thing (too fragile), so I used A/C ply instead. I labeled every part as it came off the table with its FreeCAD part label.
- **Seat first.** I built the low-profile ACES II seat first. FLIM's original guide builds the seat back first. I preferred building it inside-out from the model: pan and inner walls, then skins, then the spine and headrest.
- **Glue plus screws.** I used FLIM's method: medium CA with accelerator on the glue joints, and screws doing the structural work. A 23-gauge pin nailer holds things while the CA grabs. You don't need an 18-gauge gun.
- **Check screw lengths against the actual stack.** In a few places, the screw length you'd guess from the plans barely bites into the part behind. Measure the real stack thickness before you drive anything.
- **Compare the model and the DXF.** A handful of part outlines differ between the FreeCAD model and the DXF, and a couple of parts exist in only one of them. Compare before you cut, not after.
- **Print the fiddly bits.** I replaced several small parts (headrest pads, a lever, the ejection handles) with single PETG prints from the Prusa XL. That beat gluing up stacked layers of sheet goods, and let me add round-overs.

## What I'd change

- **Make every console separable from the seat.** I want to reach every side of the left, right and front consoles for wiring and maintenance, so none of them are permanently attached to the seat. Decide this before you glue anything.
- **Height.** On a flat floor, my side consoles sit too low relative to the seat. The plans have no riser, so I'm adding a riser box under each console.
- **Skins.** I'm adding a bent outer skin to both side consoles to close them in and make them look finished.

## Where it stands

The glare shield went together last, and the TM Warthog throttle drops into the left console. I'm waiting on the console skins and the monitor. Meanwhile the work has moved to the fun part: [acrylic panels](/posts/aux-lts-panel/), starting with AUX LTS.
