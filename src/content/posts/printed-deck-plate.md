## Why not just cut the plywood

The panel has a PCB and a printed back case behind it, so it can't sit on a flat deck. It needs a hole the shape of that back case, and something for the panel's corners to rest on and screw into. The [corner-trim post](/posts/aux-lts-trimmed-back-case/) covers how the PCB was notched to leave room for those ears.

I tried cutting that into the plywood plate that was already there. It's a small part with small features, and the bits that had to come out were tricky. A printer is good at exactly that kind of shape.

## What the print does

It has the same outline as the plywood plate it replaces, so it drops into the console the same way. Beyond that:

- **The AUX LTS opening**, following the back of the panel, with ears under the corners.
- **Heat-set threaded inserts** for the panel screws. The panel can come out and go back in as often as it needs to without chewing up wood.
- **Ribs underneath.** The plate is only supported at its front and rear edges, so the long sides need stiffening.
- **Screws at the rear** as well as the front, into the plywood it rests on.
- **A second opening** ahead of the AUX LTS, for the seat and emergency brake panel. That panel isn't built yet, but the plate was the time to make room for it.

## Checking before the long print

Before committing to the full part I printed a thin template of its outline and laid it on the console. The fit looked right, as far as a thin sheet can tell you, and the full-thickness part went ahead from there.

## Where this leaves the left console

One panel is in its real home. It's the same panel that ran in DCS on the bench through the [carrier PCB](/posts/mega-carrier-first-power/), so the next step for it is doing that from inside the console.

The forward opening is waiting for the seat and emergency brake panel.
