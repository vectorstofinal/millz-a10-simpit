<div class="bottom-line">This is the build log for my A-10C Warthog sim pit. Every setting, test result and file I make goes here, free to use. Where my work stands on someone else's, I credit them, and I only post their files when they've released them for reuse.</div>

I fly the Hog in DCS. At the end of 2023 I started building a cockpit for it in my shop, millz, which already does CNC routing, laser work, 3D printing and small electronics. The wooden structure is done. Now I'm into the part that takes real time: dozens of backlit acrylic panels, the boards behind them, and the controller boxes that connect it all to the sim.

Most of what I know came from other builders sharing their work, especially [The Warthog Project](https://thewarthogproject.com). This site is me returning the favor.

## What's here

- **[Build log](/):** what I did, what went wrong, and what I'd tell myself next time. Bottom line first.
- **[Files](/files/):** DXFs, LightBurn jobs, PCB milling files, STLs, OpenSCAD source and Arduino sketches.
- **[Settings](/settings/):** one page with every laser, router and printer setting I've proven, so you don't have to dig through posts.
- **[3D](/3d/aux-lts/):** interactive models of the panels as built.

## What's free, and what isn't

My original work (settings, test results, board designs, printed parts, code) is released **CC0**. Copy it, change it, sell it. No credit needed.

The panel files are my edits of The Warthog Project's release. Its author put those files out to the public to do with as you will, and I'm passing that on with credit.

The cockpit **structure** comes from FLIM's paid Vr-pits plans. I write about building it, but I don't post his files or anything derived from them. If you want the same structure, buy them from FLIM.

Some things I use but don't own: Les Green's printable parts on GrabCAD, DCS cockpit textures, commercial knobs. For those I link to the source instead of hosting a copy. The [about page](/about/#credits) lists everyone.

## Where it stands

Seat, side consoles and glare shield are built. The first panel, **AUX LTS**, has its base and middle layers cut, the top layer painted, and its board coupon milled. Start with the [panel post](/posts/aux-lts-panel/) or the [laser settings](/posts/acrylic-laser-settings/).

Check sizes against your own parts before you cut. Mine are measured on my switches, my acrylic and my machines.
